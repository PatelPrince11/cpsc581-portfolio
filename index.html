02Design philosophy · v2, November 2026

What I believe about design, so far.

Each principle came from a real decision in a project and links back to it. I revise the list after every project and record the changes in the growth log.

v2 keeps all five v1 principles, widens 01 and 03, adds an example to 05 and adds two new ones from testing TiltTwin on real phones.

  1. 01Design how the parts relate. That includes the people.

    Four of our five Project 1 concepts put the four of us side by side, and none of them could show how we related, which was what the brief asked for. Now I look at how the elements of an interface act on each other before I polish any one of them. TiltTwin pushed this further: it's two people on two devices, and the expert's live view of the beginner mattered as much as the beginner's own screen.

    From Project 1 · The pattern and Project 2 · Wireframes

  2. 02Go wide while ideas are cheap.

    Five concepts on paper cost us an evening. Building the wrong one would have cost us the week. Having several ideas side by side also let us compare them, and that's how we spotted the flaw they all shared. In Project 2, ten sketches let us drop our first idea before we built any of it.

    From Project 1 · Diverge and Project 2 · Converge

  3. 03Be honest with the data and the evidence, even when it's boring.

    If two people share no hobby, the interface says so. If the personality match is 37%, it shows 37%. Once people catch an interface inventing connections, they stop trusting the rest of it. The same goes for my own results: three people finishing a task in about 4 seconds shows that it works, not that it beats a mouse, so that's all I claim.

    From Project 1 · Data honesty and Project 2 · Testing

  4. 04Constraints are design material.

    We had one week, four schedules and nobody who could animate. The puzzle and mountain concepts looked great on paper, but we couldn't have built them in time, so we dropped them. I count what can actually be built as part of the design. In Project 2, limiting each lesson to one axis made the hard part buildable in a week.

    From Project 1 · Converge and Project 2 · Build

  5. 05Build accessibility in from the start.

    A drag-and-drop toy still has to work with a keyboard, on a phone and for people who turn off animation. I list those as requirements next to the features, before anything gets built. TiltTwin has a drag fallback for phones without motion sensors, 44 px tap targets, and sound, vibration and colour together, so no single sense carries the feedback.

    From Project 1 · Build and Project 2 · Build

  6. 06Words are interface. Test them in a real hand.

    Our Y-axis hint passed every unit test and still failed on a real iPhone. "Turn left" means two things depending on whether you watch the phone or the screen. Naming a physical edge of the device fixed it. Now I describe a motion by a landmark, not a direction word, and I test the wording with a person.

    New in v2, from Project 2 · Testing

  7. 07Watch what people do after they succeed.

    After the "Fits!" message, all three pilot participants relaxed their hands, and the expert's view drifted away from the target. We never saw that in walkthroughs. Success isn't the end of the interaction, so I now watch what happens next. This one led to the snap-on-target fix.

    New in v2, from Project 2 · Testing

03Growth log

How my practice is changing.

What I learned at each milestone, what I'd do differently, and how my principles changed.

  1. September 2026

    Project 1: A Family of Buttons

    My first full round of going wide, then narrowing down. The lesson: we spent most of our concept time on what each person looked like and almost none on how people relate, until we wrote down our criteria. Next time I'll write the criteria before anyone sketches.

  2. October 2026

    Portfolio v1 and design philosophy v1

    Built this site and wrote five starting principles, each tied to a decision in Project 1.

  3. Oct 15, 2026

    Check-in 1

    Portfolio with Project 1 and my own contributions to it.

  4. November 2026

    Project 2: TiltTwin

    The biggest change was when I test. Our Y-axis hint passed every unit test and still failed the first time I held a real phone. The maths was right and the words were wrong. The pilot did the same to us: people relaxed their hands after "Fits!", which we never saw in walkthroughs. Now I put the real device in a real hand as early as I can, and I treat instructions as part of the interface. I also learned to say what my evidence doesn't show. Three people finishing in about 4 seconds proves tilt works, not that it beats a mouse.

  5. Nov 19, 2026

    Check-in 2 and design philosophy v2

    Added the Project 2 case study. The principles are now v2: two widened, one extended with an example and two new ones from testing.

  6. Dec 4, 2026

    Final portfolio

    Project 3, the final version of my principles and a look back across all three projects.

04About

05AI use & human contributions

How I used AI.

Building this portfolio

I used Claude Code (Claude Opus 5.5) as a development assistant to build this portfolio. I led the design, made the main decisions, selected the content and decided how my projects should be presented. I directed the work, reviewed and refined the generated code, and made the final calls on the site's structure and presentation.

How I used AI

Claude Code helped write and refine the HTML, CSS and JavaScript, draft first versions of the project descriptions from our Project 1 and Project 2 reports, pull out the project figures and capture screenshots of the live Project 1 app. I provided the direction and the source material, reviewed the output, made revisions and checked the content against the original reports and apps before publishing.

On the course projects

Project 1. I used Claude Code (Claude Sonnet 5.5) to help develop the app. I directed the work and reviewed the result. Case study

Project 2 (TiltTwin). Our team used Claude and ChatGPT for implementation, project planning, debugging and documentation.

  • Claude helped implement parts of the v2 prototype: the phone-orientation hook, the sync layer and the feedback UI. It also helped with unit tests, screenshots and a first draft of the design-process report.
  • I used ChatGPT to organize the project into ten GitHub issues, set implementation priorities and dependencies, and get frontend guidance and debugging help during the baseline prototype.
  • ChatGPT also helped review and refine the final design-process report: improving technical explanations, documenting test results and preparing HTML revisions that kept the report's layout.

Our team reviewed AI-generated suggestions and code before using them. We stayed responsible for the design decisions, implementation choices, testing and the final submitted work. Case study

Made by people

The core design work, creative direction and project decisions came from our team.

  • All concept sketches, storyboards and wireframes were created by our team.
  • The core concepts, design directions and major project decisions were ours.
  • Our team selected and put together the hobby lists and ITP personality results the Project 1 app uses.
  • For TiltTwin, our team did the real-device testing, ran the three-person pilot and decided what to change based on the feedback.
