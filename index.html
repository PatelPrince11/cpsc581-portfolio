02Design philosophy · v1, October 2026

What I believe about design, so far.

These principles came out of real decisions in my projects, and each one links back to the moment it came from. I'll revise this list after every project, and the growth log records what changed.

  1. 01Design the relationships, not just the parts.

    Four of our five concepts showed four people side by side, and none of them could show how those people related. The interesting part of an interface is often in how its elements act on each other.

    From Project 1 · The pattern

  2. 02Go wide while ideas are cheap.

    Sketching five concepts on paper cost us an evening. Building the wrong one would have cost us the week. Parallel ideas also gave us something to compare, so we found the pattern by contrast.

    From Project 1 · Diverge

  3. 03Be honest with the data, even when it's boring.

    If two people share no hobby, the interface says so. If the personality match is only 37%, it shows 37%. An interface that invents connections loses the user's trust.

    From Project 1 · Data honesty

  4. 04Constraints are design material.

    We had one week, four schedules and no animators. We dropped the beautiful puzzle and mountain concepts because they couldn't ship. Feasibility doesn't compromise a design. It's part of the design.

    From Project 1 · Converge

  5. 05Accessible by default, not by patch.

    A playful drag-and-drop interface still has to work with a keyboard, on a phone, and for people who turn off motion. I treat these as requirements from the start.

    From Project 1 · Build

03Growth log

  1. September 2026

    Project 1: A Family of Buttons

    My first proper divergent-then-convergent process. Main lesson: we spent most of our concept time on what each person looks like and almost none on how people relate, until we stated our design criteria. Next time I'll write the criteria before sketching.
