02Design philosophy · v1, October 2026

What I believe about design, so far.

Each principle came from a real decision in a project and links back to it. I'll revise the list after every project and record the changes in the growth log.

  1. 01Design how the parts relate.

    Four of our five Project 1 concepts put the four of us side by side, and none of them could show how we related, which was what the brief asked for. Now I look at how the elements of an interface act on each other before I polish any one of them.

    From Project 1 · The pattern

  2. 02Go wide while ideas are cheap.

    Five concepts on paper cost us an evening. Building the wrong one would have cost us the week. Having several ideas side by side also let us compare them, and that's how we spotted the flaw they all shared.

    From Project 1 · Diverge

  3. 03Be honest with the data, even when it's boring.

    If two people share no hobby, the interface says so. If the personality match is 37%, it shows 37%. Once people catch an interface inventing connections, they stop trusting the rest of it.

    From Project 1 · Data honesty

  4. 04Constraints are design material.

    We had one week, four schedules and nobody who could animate. The puzzle and mountain concepts looked great on paper, but we couldn't have built them in time, so we dropped them. I count what can actually be built as part of the design.

    From Project 1 · Converge

  5. 05Build accessibility in from the start.

    A drag-and-drop toy still has to work with a keyboard, on a phone and for people who turn off animation. I list those as requirements next to the features, before anything gets built.

    From Project 1 · Build

    Project 1: A Family of Buttons

    My first full round of going wide, then narrowing down. The lesson: we spent most of our concept time on what each person looked like and almost none on how people relate, until we wrote down our criteria. Next time I'll write the criteria before anyone sketches.

05AI use

How I used AI.

I used Claude Code (Claude Opus 5.5) to help build this portfolio. I led the design, made the main decisions, chose the content and decided how the project is presented.

What Claude did

It wrote and revised the HTML, CSS and JavaScript, drafted first versions of the text from our Project 1 report, pulled the figures out of the report and took the screenshots of the live app. I reviewed and edited what it produced, and checked it against the original report and app before publishing.

The Project 1 app was also built with Claude Code (Claude Sonnet 5.5). The case study covers how.
